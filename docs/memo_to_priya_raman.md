# Vireo Audio — Weekly Support Memo

**To:** Priya Raman  
**Period:** 22–28 June 2026  
**Subject:** Support volume, repeat contacts, and customer experience

## Executive summary

Support volume increased to **199 tickets**, up **19.2%** from the previous complete week (167). The main areas of concern were **Delivery & Shipping, Charging & Battery, and Other**.

We identified **23 potential repeat contacts**, representing **11.6% of the week's tickets** and approximately **₹6,150 in estimated contact cost**. These are customers who contacted support again within 30 days about the same customer/product/category combination; this is a screening measure rather than a confirmed same-issue rate.

## What changed

- **199 tickets**, up **19.2% week over week**
- **23 potential repeat contacts** — 11.6%
- **15 first-response SLA breaches** — 7.5%, representing **₹5,250** in policy exposure
- **CSAT: 3.31/5**
- **21 internal transfers**
- **30 refund cases**, totaling approximately **₹1.26 lakh**

The largest week-over-week category increases were:

- **Charging & Battery:** +14 tickets
- **Returns & Refunds:** +11
- **Audio Quality:** +6

## Where customers are repeating

The largest number of potential repeat contacts came from **Other (7)**, followed by **Delivery & Shipping (4)** and **Returns & Refunds (3)**.

Across the broader dataset, potential repeat contacts also show a relationship with lower customer satisfaction: the observed CSAT was approximately **2.42 for potential repeats versus 3.43 for other tickets**. This is an association, not proof that repeat contact causes lower CSAT.

## Recommended actions

1. **Investigate repeat-contact cases first**, particularly Delivery, Returns, and the “Other” category, to identify issues that require customers to contact support again.
2. **Review the Charging & Battery increase** from this week and determine whether there is a product, troubleshooting, or delivery-related pattern.
3. **Watch first-response SLA performance**, especially because each breach carries a ₹350 store-credit exposure under the current policy.

## Important interpretation

The repeat-contact figure should be treated as a **candidate signal**, not a confirmed failure rate. The available ticket data does not contain a definitive “same issue” label, so the analysis intentionally avoids claiming that all 23 cases are genuine repeats.

**Bottom line:** The immediate opportunity is to reduce avoidable repeat contacts while addressing the sharp weekly increase in Charging & Battery and Returns cases. Doing so could reduce queue demand, improve customer experience, and avoid the associated contact and SLA costs.
